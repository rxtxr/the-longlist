# Gutachten: Copywriter & Agentur-Historiker

# Kritik: Lexikon zur Werbegeschichte
**Von einem Creative Director, der die Asche noch im Hals hat**

Ich habe 25 Jahre in diesem Business verbrannt. Hamburg, Frankfurt, selbstständig. Ich habe Pitch-N Nächte überlebt, in denen der Kaffee kalt und die Hoffnung dünn wurde. Ich habe Kunden seen, die Konzepte kippten, weil ihre Frau das Logo nicht mag. Ich habe Bücher geschrieben, weil ich wusste: Die Legende im Jahresbericht ist nicht die Wahrheit.

Wenn ich dieses Lexikon lese, riecht ich oft das Papier, aber nicht den Tabak. Ich sehe die Daten, aber nicht das Blut. Hier ist my assessment. Nicht akademisch. Ehrlich.

---

## 1. Stimmt die Geschichte?
**Legenden vs. Wirklichkeit**

Viele entries lesen sich wie die Pressetexte, die die Agenturen selbst nach einem successful Pitch verschickt haben. Clean. Polished. Safe.
*   **Das Problem mit den "[ungesichert]"-Tags:** 48% der Artikel haben low confidence. In einem historischen Lexikon? Das ist wie ein Architekt, der sagt: „Das Haus steht vielleicht, wissen wir nicht." Geschichte braucht Haltung. Wenn you don't know, don't write it. Or write it as a rumour, not as a fact with a warning label.
*   **Missing the Pain:** Die Geschichte der Werbung ist auch eine Geschichte des Scheitens. Entry [3] BBDO Düsseldorf klingt wie ein Erfolgsreport. Was fehlt? Die interne Zerrissenheit bei der Fusion 1986. Die Creative Directors, die gefeuert wurden, weil die Billings nicht stimmten. Die Angst, den nexten großen Kunden zu verlieren.
*   **The Real Moments:** Entry [1] Geigy ist überraschend gut. Die interne „Propaganda"-Abteilung? Das ist realer als jede external agency. Das war der Moment, wo Corporate Identity noch Handwerk war, nicht PowerPoint.
*   **Wikipedia-Feel:** Entry [4] Grey Germany. Dry. „Kulturelle Übersetzungsleistung". klingt wie ein Uni-Seminar. Die Wirklichkeit war: Amerikaner kamen nach Düsseldorf, traten lokale Talente vor den Kopf und nannten es Strategie.

## 2. Ton und Haltung
**Voice or Vacuum?**

*   **Neutral bis leblos:** Too many articles start with „X was more than just..." (Entry [1], [2], [3], [4], [8], [13], [14], [16], [17], [18], [20], [21], [22]). Das ist ein Klaxon. If you say it every time, it means nothing.
*   **Where it breathes:** Entry [14] Schneider+Schneider. „Lehrstück für den ewigen Konflikt zwischen radikaler Kreativität und wirtschaftlichem Überleben". Das klingt nach jemandem, who knows that creativity often dies on the balance sheet. Entry [5] John Webster. „Volkskünstler". „Unterbrechung des Fernsehprogramms". Das hat rhythm. Das hat love.
*   **People vs. Career Steps:** Entry [6] Frank Gehry. Why is an architect in an ad lexicon? Because he built buildings? That's weak. Entry [17] Tim Delaney. „Hotelpage become Bot". That's a human story. Entry [12] Sexism. Finally, some grit. „Casting-Couch". „Gender tax". Das ist not neutral. Das is necessary. But it feels too safe, too statistical. I want the names of the men who did it. I want the specific meetings where women were told to shut up.

## 3. Branchenkenntnis
**Deep Understanding or Half-Knowledge?**

*   **Surface Strategy:** Entry [11] Rosser Reeves. USP is explained correctly, but the *pressure* is missing. Reeves was a dictator. His hard sell was not just a concept, it was a weapon. The article feels like a definition, not a experience.
*   **The Media Split:** Entry [26] Media-Agenturen Trennung. Crucial topic. But it reads like a business school case. The reality: Creative Directors lost power. Money moved to media buyers. The art died, the algorithm born. The article misses the tragedy of this shift.
*   **Surprise:** Entry [23] Werbebranche in der Schweiz. Good niche knowledge. Wirz, Ruf Lanz. That's insider stuff for the German-speaking region. Most lexicons ignore the Swiss precision that actually shaped global design.
*   **Gap:** Production. Entry [8] Werbefilm-Produktion. Mentions Bulova 1941. But misses the chaos of the shoot. The director yelling, the client changing the script at 5 AM. The magic is in the mess, not in the timeline.

## 4. Das System als Ganzes
**The Blind Spots**

*   **Geography:** Too US/UK focused, even when talking German agencies. The German client culture (conservative, industrial, risk-averse) is the real antagonist in many German ad stories. It's missing.
*   **Time:** The 2000s death of big agencies (my second book topic) is hinted at in Entry [26], but not felt. The consolidation, the stock market pressure, the creative exhaustion.
*   **Stance:** The lexicon is too respectful. Advertising is not a noble profession. It's commerce with art school degrees. It's seduction. It's manipulation. The lexicon treats it like a museum exhibit. I want to see the fingerprints on the glass.
*   **Category Errors:** Entry [20] Jessica Livingston (VC) and [6] Gehry (Architect). Why? If this is an ad lexicon, keep it tight. VC is funding, not ad. Architecture is building, not ad. Unless it's about branding, but then make that clear. It feels like filling gaps with famous names.

## 5. Die drei besten und die drei schwächsten Artikel

### **Top 3**
1.  **[14] Schneider+Schneider:** Because it acknowledges the tragedy. „Radical creativity vs. economic survival". That's the real story of every German agency in the 90s. It feels written by someone who saw them burn out.
2.  **[5] John Webster:** Captures the spirit of the Creative Revolution without the clichés. „Volkskünstler". Understands that ads are entertainment first, sales second.
3.  **[12] Sexism in Werbeagenturen:** Brave. Necessary. Doesn't shy away from the „toxic mix". It's the only entry that admits the industry was often a hostile place for half the population.

### **Weak 3**
1.  **[6] Frank Gehry:** Category error. Why is he here? Unless it's about corporate architecture branding, it's noise. Distracts from the core subject.
2.  **[4] Grey Germany:** Too many "[ungesichert]" tags. „Sources are thin". Then why write it? Feels like a placeholder. Dry, corporate language. No blood.
3.  **[15] Mediaagentur:** Definition dry. „Strategic Navigator". Sounds like a LinkedIn profile. Misses the power shift, the loss of creative control, the cold hard data that killed the romance.

## 6. Abschluss: Recommendation?

**Would I recommend this to a young copywriter?**

**Yes, but with a warning label.**

It's a map, not the terrain. It gives them the names, the dates, the structure. It tells them who Bill Bernbach was. That's important. You need to know your ancestors.

But I would tell them: „Read this to know the legends. Then go find the people who are still alive and ask them about the nights they cried in the office."

The lexicon is safe. Advertising history is not safe. It's messy, human, and often unfair. If this book teaches them the facts, good. But I hope they don't mistake these clean entries for the reality of the job. The reality is in the pitch night, not in the lexicon.

**Rating:** 3.5 out of 5. Good foundation. Needs more smoke, more fire, less uncertainty.
